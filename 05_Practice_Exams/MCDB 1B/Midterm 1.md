## Section 2: True/False

**13.** The fovea of the human retina contains a high density of rod photoreceptors.
**FALSE** — The fovea is dense with **cones**, providing high-acuity color vision.

**14.** Positive feedback loops are responsible for maintaining homeostasis.
**FALSE** — Negative feedback maintains homeostasis; positive feedback amplifies changes away from the set point.

**15.** The AV node introduces a brief delay in cardiac conduction to allow the ventricles to fill with blood before contracting.
**TRUE**

**16.** During repolarization of the action potential, voltage-gated K⁺ channels open and K⁺ flows out of the cell.
**TRUE**

**17.** Rods provide color vision but function poorly in dim light, while cones work well in low-light conditions.
**FALSE** — Rods work in dim light (no color); cones require bright light and provide color vision.

## Section 3: Short Answer

**18.** Describe what would happen to a muscle if the Ca²⁺ pump in the sarcoplasmic reticulum stopped working. Specify what would happen to the molecular pathway and what the muscle would do. (3 points)

*Model Answer:* If the Ca²⁺ pump stopped, Ca²⁺ would remain elevated in the cytoplasm instead of being returned to the SR. Ca²⁺ would continue to bind troponin, keeping tropomyosin shifted and myosin-binding sites exposed. Cross-bridge cycling would continue uncontrollably, resulting in sustained, uncontrolled muscle contraction (tetanic contraction). The muscle would be unable to relax.

---

**19.** A classmate tells you that the sodium-potassium pump makes the inside of the neuron negative because it pumps more Na⁺ out than K⁺ in. Is this correct? Explain the ionic basis of the resting membrane potential. (3 points)

*Model Answer:* Partially correct. The Na⁺/K⁺ pump does contribute to the negative inside by pumping 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in (net positive charge removed). However, the resting potential is primarily maintained by the selective permeability of the membrane — K⁺ leak channels allow K⁺ to exit down its concentration gradient, leaving behind negative charges. The combination of this K⁺ leak and the pump establishes the −70 mV resting potential.
