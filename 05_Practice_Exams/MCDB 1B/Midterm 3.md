## Section 2: True/False

**13.** The dermal tissue system in plants includes the xylem and phloem.
**FALSE** — Dermal tissue is the outer protective layer. Xylem and phloem are vascular tissue.

**14.** In phloem transport, flow goes from source (high sugar) to sink (low sugar), driven by a pressure gradient.
**TRUE**

**15.** The descending limb of the Loop of Henle is permeable to water but impermeable to ions.
**TRUE**

**16.** CAM plants separate C4 and Calvin cycle reactions spatially into two different cell types.
**FALSE** — CAM plants separate them **temporally** (CO₂ fixation at night; Calvin cycle during day). C4 plants use spatial separation.

**17.** Abscisic acid (ABA) causes stomata to open, increasing transpiration during drought.
**FALSE** — ABA causes stomata to **close** to prevent water loss during drought.

## Section 3: Short Answer

**18.** Draw and explain the pressure-flow model of phloem transport. Include: source vs. sink, how sucrose enters and exits, and what drives the flow. (4 points)

*Model Answer:* At the **source** (photosynthesizing leaf), sucrose is actively loaded into sieve tube elements via H⁺-ATPase-driven co-transport. The high sucrose concentration causes water to enter by osmosis, generating **high turgor pressure**. At the **sink** (root, fruit, growing tip), sucrose is unloaded and used or stored. Water then exits the phloem (returning to xylem or tissues), creating **low pressure** at the sink. This pressure difference drives mass flow of phloem sap from source (high P) to sink (low P). The loading step is active (requires energy); the actual flow is passive (pressure-driven).

**19.** Explain why RuBisCo causes problems for C3 plants in hot, dry environments, and contrast how C4 and CAM plants solve this problem differently. (4 points)

*Model Answer:* RuBisCo can react with either CO₂ (carbon fixation) or O₂ (photorespiration — wasteful). In hot, dry conditions, plants close stomata to reduce water loss, causing CO₂ to drop and O₂ to rise inside the leaf → RuBisCo increasingly fixes O₂ → photorespiration increases, wasting ATP and reducing photosynthetic efficiency. **C4 plants** solve this spatially: mesophyll cells pre-fix CO₂ into a 4C compound (oxaloacetate), which is transported to bundle sheath cells. There, CO₂ is released in high concentration near RuBisCo, suppressing O₂ reactions. Example: corn, sugarcane. **CAM plants** solve this temporally: stomata open at night to fix CO₂ into 4C acids stored in vacuoles; during the day (stomata closed), CO₂ is released from the acids and fixed by the Calvin cycle. Example: cacti, succulents, pineapple.
