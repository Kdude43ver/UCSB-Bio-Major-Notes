# Resting Membrane Potential

**Course:** [[Course Dashboard]] | **Yield:** HIGH ⭐⭐⭐

---

## Definition

The resting membrane potential (RMP) is the voltage difference across a neuron's plasma membrane at rest: approximately **−70 mV** (inside negative relative to outside). It results from unequal distribution of ions and selective membrane permeability.

## Mechanism

**Key ions and their contributions:**

| Ion | Concentration | Direction of gradient | Contribution to RMP |
|-----|--------------|----------------------|---------------------|
| K⁺ | High inside | Leaks out | Makes inside more negative |
| Na⁺ | High outside | Would enter | Largely excluded at rest |
| Cl⁻ | High outside | Equilibrated | Minor role |
| A⁻ (organic anions) | Trapped inside | Cannot cross | Stabilizes negative interior |

**Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase:**
- Pumps **3 Na⁺ out / 2 K⁺ in** per cycle (electrogenic — net outward positive charge)
- Maintains ion gradients; contributes ~−3 mV to RMP directly
- Requires ATP; inhibited by ouabain

**Equilibrium potential (Nernst equation):**
- E_K ≈ −90 mV (K⁺ is main driver of RMP)
- E_Na ≈ +60 mV
- RMP (~−70 mV) reflects higher permeability to K⁺ than Na⁺ at rest

## Biological Role

RMP is the "loaded spring" that enables rapid electrical signaling. The electrochemical gradients established at rest power action potential generation. Without RMP, neurons and muscle cells cannot fire.
