# Phototransduction

**Course:** [[Course Dashboard]] | **Yield:** MEDIUM ⭐⭐

---

## Definition

Phototransduction is the conversion of light energy into an electrical signal in photoreceptor cells (rods and cones) of the retina. It relies on a G-protein signaling cascade triggered by photon absorption by the visual pigment rhodopsin (or opsin variants in cones).

## Mechanism

### Photoreceptor Types

| Property | Rods | Cones |
|----------|------|-------|
| Pigment | Rhodopsin (1 type) | Photopsins (3 types: S, M, L) |
| Function | Dim light / night vision | Color vision / high acuity |
| Distribution | Periphery | Fovea |
| Sensitivity | Extremely sensitive | Less sensitive |

### Dark State (no light)
1. cGMP phosphodiesterase is **inactive** (high [cGMP])
2. cGMP-gated **Na⁺/Ca²⁺ channels** are **open** → depolarized photoreceptor
3. Continuous **glutamate release** onto bipolar cells (synaptic terminal active)

### Light Signal (phototransduction cascade)
1. **Photon** absorbed by **retinal** (11-*cis* → all-*trans* isomerization)
2. **Rhodopsin** (R) activated → **R*** (opsin + all-trans retinal)
3. **Transducin** (G protein, α subunit) activated → activates **phosphodiesterase (PDE)**
4. PDE hydrolyzes **cGMP → 5'-GMP** → [cGMP] drops
5. cGMP-gated channels **close** → **hyperpolarization** of photoreceptor
6. **Glutamate release stops** → signal to bipolar cell

### Recovery
- **Arrestin** binds R* → inactivates rhodopsin
- **RGS proteins** inactivate transducin (GTPase acceleration)
- **Retinal regeneration**: all-trans retinal transported to RPE → re-isomerized to 11-cis

**Key concept:** Light causes *hyperpolarization* of rods (counterintuitive — opposite of most sensory cells).
