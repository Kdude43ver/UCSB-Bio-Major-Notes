# Sliding Filament Model

## Definition
The mechanism by which muscle contraction occurs: thick (myosin) filaments pull thin (actin) filaments toward the center of the sarcomere, shortening the sarcomere without changing filament length.

## Mechanism (Cross-Bridge Cycle)
1. Ca²⁺ binds troponin → tropomyosin shifts → myosin-binding sites on actin exposed
2. Myosin head (low-energy) binds actin
3. **ATP binds myosin** → myosin releases actin
4. **ATP hydrolyzed** (ADP + Pᵢ) → myosin cocks to high-energy configuration
5. Myosin re-attaches to new site on actin; **ADP + Pᵢ released** → **power stroke** (sarcomere shortens)
6. Cycle repeats as long as Ca²⁺ and ATP are present

## Key Components
- **Actin** (thin filament): Contains troponin-tropomyosin regulatory proteins
- **Myosin** (thick filament): Has ATPase heads that bind actin and generate force
- **Troponin:** Ca²⁺-binding protein; shifts tropomyosin when Ca²⁺ binds
- **Tropomyosin:** Blocks myosin-binding sites at rest
- **Ca²⁺:** Released from sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR) upon neural stimulation
- **ATP:** Required for (1) myosin release from actin and (2) Ca²⁺ reuptake into SR

## Biological Role
- Generates force for all voluntary and involuntary movement
- Powers breathing, locomotion, cardiac pumping, peristalsis

## Importance: HIGH ⭐⭐⭐
The cross-bridge cycle steps and the specific role of ATP at each step (release vs. cocking) are Midterm 1 favorites. Rigor mortis (no ATP → myosin stuck to actin) is a common application question.
