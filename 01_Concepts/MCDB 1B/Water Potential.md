# Water Potential (Ψ)

## Definition
The potential energy of water relative to pure water; determines the direction of water movement. Water always moves from **higher Ψ to lower Ψ**.

## Mechanism
**Ψ = Ψs + Ψp**

- **Ψs (solute potential):** Always negative. More solutes = lower Ψs = lower Ψ.
  - Formula: Ψs = −iCRT (or −RTcS)
- **Ψp (pressure potential):** Can be positive (turgor pressure — cell wall pushing back on water) or negative (tension in xylem).
- **Pure water:** Ψ = 0 (reference point)

## Key Rules
- Water moves from HIGH Ψ → LOW Ψ
- Adding solutes → decreases Ψ (lowers Ψs)
- Applying pressure → increases Ψ (increases Ψp)
- In xylem: Ψp is **negative** (tension) due to transpiration pull → water pulled upward

## Biological Role
- Drives water from soil → roots → xylem → leaves
- Controls cell turgor pressure and plant cell shape/structure
- Governs stomatal opening/closing
- Drives osmosis across membranes

## Key Application: Water Flow in Roots
1. Soil has higher Ψ than root cells (more solutes in root)
2. Water flows into root by osmosis
3. Casparian strip forces symplastic entry → selective mineral uptake
4. Water enters xylem and is pulled upward by transpiration
