# MHC Classes (Major Histocompatibility Complex)

## Definition
Cell-surface proteins that present peptide antigens to T cells; the molecular basis of self/non-self discrimination in the adaptive immune system.

## Mechanism

### MHC Class I
- **Expressed on:** ALL nucleated cells in the body
- **Presents:** Intracellular peptides (endogenous — from proteins made inside the cell)
  - e.g., viral peptides, cancer cell abnormal proteins
- **Recognized by:** Cytotoxic T cells (CD8⁺)
- **Outcome:** Cytotoxic T cell kills the infected/abnormal cell

### MHC Class II
- **Expressed on:** Antigen-presenting cells ONLY (APCs): macrophages, dendritic cells, B cells
- **Presents:** Extracellular peptides (exogenous — from phagocytosed pathogens)
  - e.g., bacterial peptides
- **Recognized by:** Helper T cells (CD4⁺)
- **Outcome:** Helper T cell activates B cells → antibody production

## Key Components
- MHC molecules: transmembrane glycoproteins
- **Epitope:** The specific antigen fragment that fits into the MHC binding groove
- T cell receptor (TCR): Recognizes the MHC-antigen complex

## Biological Role
- Central to distinguishing "self" cells from "non-self" / infected cells
- Coordinates both humoral (antibody) and cellular immune responses
- Basis for transplant rejection: Foreign MHC I on donor cells triggers host CTL response

## Transplant Rejection
- Recipient's cytotoxic T cells recognize **foreign MHC I** on donor organ
- Identify donor cells as "non-self" → attack and destroy
- Immunosuppressive drugs (e.g., cyclosporine) must be taken to prevent rejection
