# Blood Pressure Regulation

**Course:** [[Course Dashboard]] | **Yield:** MEDIUM ⭐⭐

---

## Definition

Blood pressure is regulated through short-term (neural/baroreceptor) and long-term (hormonal/renal) mechanisms that adjust cardiac output and peripheral resistance. Blood pressure = Cardiac Output × Total Peripheral Resistance.

## Mechanism

### Short-Term: Baroreceptor Reflex
1. **Baroreceptors** (carotid sinus, aortic arch) detect stretch (↑ BP)
2. Signal to medulla oblongata (cardiovascular center)
3. Response: ↓ sympathetic → ↓ HR, ↓ contractility; ↑ parasympathetic (vagus) → ↓ HR
4. Peripheral arterioles dilate → ↓ resistance → BP returns to set point

### Long-Term: Renin-Angiotensin-Aldosterone System (RAAS)
```
↓ BP / ↓ [Na⁺] / ↑ sympathetic
    ↓
Kidney juxtaglomerular cells → Renin released
    ↓
Angiotensinogen → Angiotensin I (ACE in lung) → Angiotensin II
    ↓                              ↓
Vasoconstriction              Adrenal cortex
(↑ resistance)                    ↓
                            Aldosterone → Na⁺ reabsorption → ↑ blood volume → ↑ BP
```

**ADH contribution:**
- ↑ osmolarity → posterior pituitary → ADH release → AQP-2 insertion in CD → water reabsorption → ↑ blood volume → ↑ BP

**ANP (opposes RAAS):**
- ↑ atrial stretch (↑ BP) → ANP from atria → ↑ GFR, ↓ Na⁺ reabsorption → ↓ blood volume → ↓ BP

### Key Variables

| Parameter | Relation to BP |
|-----------|---------------|
| Heart rate | Direct (↑ HR → ↑ CO → ↑ BP) |
| Stroke volume | Direct |
| Peripheral resistance | Direct (arteriole diameter) |
| Blood volume | Direct |
