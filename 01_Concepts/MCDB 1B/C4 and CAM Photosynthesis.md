# C4 and CAM Photosynthesis

**Course:** [[Course Dashboard]] | **Yield:** HIGH ⭐⭐⭐

## Definition

C4 and CAM are evolutionary adaptations to the problem of photorespiration, which occurs when RuBisCo fixes O₂ instead of CO₂ in conditions of high temperature, high light, and limited water. Both strategies use a CO₂-concentrating mechanism (CCM) to increase CO₂ around RuBisCo and suppress photorespiration.

## Mechanism

### C3 (reference — standard)
- CO₂ fixed directly by RuBisCo in mesophyll
- First product: 3-carbon molecule (3-PGA)
- Problem: photorespiration is significant at high temps

### C4 Plants (spatial separation)
**Where:** Corn, sugarcane, sorghum, amaranth

**Kranz anatomy:** Two-cell system
- **Mesophyll cells**: fix CO₂ via PEP carboxylase (PEPcase) → OAA (4C) → malate
- **Bundle sheath cells**: malate transported here → decarboxylated → releases CO₂ → enters Calvin cycle (RuBisCo surrounded by high [CO₂])

**Key advantage:** PEPcase has much higher CO₂ affinity than RuBisCo and does not react with O₂ → almost no photorespiration even at high temperatures.

**Energy cost:** Requires extra 2 ATP per CO₂ fixed (total ~5 ATP vs. ~3 in C3).

### CAM Plants (temporal separation)
**Where:** Cacti, succulents (Crassulaceae), agave, pineapple

**Strategy:**
- **Night**: Stomata open → CO₂ fixed by PEPcase → stored as malic acid in vacuole
- **Day**: Stomata closed (↓ water loss) → malate decarboxylated → CO₂ released → Calvin cycle runs

| Feature | C3 | C4 | CAM |
|---------|----|----|-----|
| CO₂ fixation enzyme | RuBisCo | PEPcase (mesophyll) then RuBisCo | PEPcase (night) then RuBisCo (day) |
| Separation | None | Spatial (cell types) | Temporal (night/day) |
| Stomata open | Day | Day | Night |
| Photorespiration | Significant | Minimal | Minimal |
| Water efficiency | Low | Medium | High |
| Examples | Wheat, rice, soybean | Corn, sugarcane | Cactus, agave |
