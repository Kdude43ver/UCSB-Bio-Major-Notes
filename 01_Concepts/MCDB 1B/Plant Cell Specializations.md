## Definition

Plant cells have unique structural features that distinguish them from animal cells and enable specialized functions in support, transport, and photosynthesis. The major additions are the cell wall, central vacuole, chloroplasts, and plasmodesmata.

## Mechanism

**Unique plant cell components:**

| Structure | Composition | Function |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| **Cell wall** | Cellulose microfibrils embedded in matrix | Support, shape, resist turgor pressure |
| **Central vacuole** | Water + dissolved solutes | Turgor pressure; storage; cell elongation |
| **Chloroplasts** | Thylakoid membranes + stroma | Photosynthesis |
| **Plasmodesmata** | Membrane-lined channels through cell walls | Symplastic transport; communication |
| **Tonoplast** | Membrane surrounding vacuole | Regulates vacuolar content |

**Cell wall layers:**
- **Primary wall**: flexible; cellulose + pectin; growing cells
- **Secondary wall**: rigid; often lignified; xylem tracheids/vessel elements

**Turgor pressure:**
- Water enters vacuole by osmosis → vacuole expands → pushes cytoplasm against wall
- **Turgor pressure (Ψp)** + **solute potential (Ψs)** = **water potential (Ψ)**
- Wilting = loss of turgor when Ψp → 0

**Plasmodesmata vs. gap junctions:**
- Both allow cell-to-cell communication
- Plasmodesmata: plant cells; pass through cell walls; lined with plasma membrane; can dilate (e.g., viral movement)
- Gap junctions: animal cells; connexin proteins; no cell wall

**Three tissue types:**
- **Dermal** (epidermis, cuticle) — protection, gas exchange via stomata
- **Ground** (parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma) — storage, support, photosynthesis
- **Vascular** (xylem, phloem) — long-distance transport
