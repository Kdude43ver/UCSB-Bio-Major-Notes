# Sarcomere Structure

**Course:** [[Course Dashboard]] | **Yield:** HIGH ⭐⭐⭐

---

## Definition

The sarcomere is the basic contractile unit of striated (skeletal and cardiac) muscle, bounded by two Z-discs. It contains precisely organized arrays of thick (myosin) and thin (actin) filaments that slide past each other during contraction.

## Mechanism

**Sarcomere anatomy:**

```
Z────────────────────────────────────Z
  |←── I band ──→|←─── A band ───→|
                 |← H zone →|
                      ↑
                     M line
```

| Band/Zone | Contents | Changes during contraction |
|-----------|----------|---------------------------|
| **A band** | Full length of thick filaments | Does **not** change length |
| **I band** | Thin filaments only (flanking Z-disc) | **Shortens** |
| **H zone** | Thick filaments only (center) | **Shortens** |
| **M line** | Center of thick filament array | Stays at center |
| **Z disc** | Anchors thin filaments | Distance between Z-discs decreases |

**Key proteins:**
- **Actin** (thin filament) + **troponin** (I, T, C subunits) + **tropomyosin**
- **Myosin** (thick filament) — head contains ATPase and actin-binding domain
- **Titin** — elastic protein connecting myosin to Z-disc; provides passive tension
- **Nebulin** — template for actin filament length

**Regulation:**
- At rest: tropomyosin covers actin-binding site → no cross-bridge formation
- Ca²⁺ binds troponin C → conformational change → tropomyosin shifts → site exposed → myosin heads bind

## Biological Role

The sarcomere translates chemical energy (ATP) into mechanical force. Sarcomere shortening → myofibril shortening → muscle fiber shortening → gross movement.
