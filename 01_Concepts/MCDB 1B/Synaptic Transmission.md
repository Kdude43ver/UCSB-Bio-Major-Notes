# Synaptic Transmission

**Course:** [[Course Dashboard]] | **Yield:** HIGH ⭐⭐⭐

## Definition

Synaptic transmission is the process by which an action potential in a presynaptic neuron triggers chemical signaling across a synapse to affect a postsynaptic cell. It converts electrical signals into chemical signals and back.

## Mechanism

**Steps of chemical synaptic transmission:**

1. **Action potential** arrives at axon terminal
2. **Voltage-gated Ca²⁺ channels** open → Ca²⁺ flows in
3. **Synaptic vesicles** fuse with presynaptic membrane (SNARE proteins)
4. **Neurotransmitters** released into synaptic cleft (exocytosis)
5. NT binds **postsynaptic receptors** → ion channels open or close
6. NT cleared: reuptake (transporter), enzymatic degradation (e.g., AChE), diffusion

**Postsynaptic potentials:**
- **EPSP** (excitatory): Na⁺/Ca²⁺ influx → depolarization (toward threshold ~−55 mV)
- **IPSP** (inhibitory): Cl⁻ influx or K⁺ efflux → hyperpolarization (away from threshold)

**Summation:**
- **Temporal summation:** rapid successive EPSPs from one neuron add up
- **Spatial summation:** simultaneous EPSPs from multiple inputs add up

## Key Neurotransmitters

| NT | Type | Action | Inactivation |
|----|------|--------|-------------|
| Acetylcholine | Excitatory (NMJ) / inhibitory (heart) | Nicotinic (ion channel) or muscarinic (GPCR) | AChE (cholinesterase) |
| Glutamate | Excitatory (CNS) | AMPA, NMDA channels | Reuptake |
| GABA | Inhibitory (CNS) | Cl⁻ channel | Reuptake |
| Dopamine | Modulatory | GPCRs | Reuptake |
| Norepinephrine | Modulatory/excitatory | GPCRs | Reuptake, MAO |

## Biological Role

Synaptic transmission enables computation in neural circuits, muscle contraction, and all rapid organismal responses. Integration of EPSPs and IPSPs at the axon hillock determines whether the postsynaptic neuron fires.
