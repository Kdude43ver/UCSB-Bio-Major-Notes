# Stomata and Guard Cells

**Course:** [[Course Dashboard]] | **Yield:** HIGH ⭐⭐⭐

---

## Definition

Stomata are microscopic pores in the leaf epidermis bounded by two guard cells that regulate gas exchange (CO₂ in, O₂ and H₂O out) and control transpiration. Opening and closing is driven by changes in guard cell turgor pressure.

## Mechanism

**Opening (light-driven):**
1. Blue light detected by phototropins in guard cells
2. H⁺-ATPase activated → protons pumped out
3. Negative membrane potential → K⁺ channels open → K⁺ flows in (also Cl⁻ and malate²⁻)
4. Osmolarity of guard cell ↑ → water enters by osmosis → guard cells swell → stoma opens

**Closing (ABA-driven, drought):**
1. ABA (abscisic acid) released when leaves wilt or roots detect soil drying
2. ABA receptor in guard cells activates signaling cascade
3. K⁺ and Cl⁻ efflux → osmolarity ↓ → water leaves → guard cells shrink → stoma closes

**Summary of signals:**

| Signal | Effect on stomata | Mechanism |
|--------|------------------|-----------|
| Blue light | Opens | H⁺-ATPase → K⁺ uptake |
| CO₂ (high) | Closes | Direct on guard cells |
| ABA | Closes | K⁺ efflux |
| Darkness | Closes | Phototropin inactive |
| Low water potential | Closes | ABA release |

**Guard cell anatomy:**
- Unevenly thickened walls: inner wall (toward pore) is thicker
- When turgid, uneven thickening causes cells to bow outward → pore opens
- Kidney-shaped in dicots; dumbbell-shaped in grasses

## Trade-off

Stomata opening lets CO₂ in for photosynthesis but also lets water vapor out (transpiration). C4 and CAM plants minimize water loss by concentrating CO₂ (C4) or fixing CO₂ at night (CAM).
