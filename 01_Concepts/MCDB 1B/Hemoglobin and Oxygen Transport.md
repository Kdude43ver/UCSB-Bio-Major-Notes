# Hemoglobin and Oxygen Transport

**Course:** [[Course Dashboard]] | **Yield:** HIGH ⭐⭐⭐

---

## Definition

Hemoglobin (Hb) is a tetrameric oxygen-transport protein in red blood cells that binds O₂ cooperatively. Its sigmoidal oxygen-dissociation curve allows it to load O₂ efficiently in the lungs and unload it in tissues.

## Mechanism

**Structure:** 4 subunits (2α + 2β in adults), each with a heme group (Fe²⁺ center binds one O₂)

**Cooperative binding:**
- Binding of O₂ to one subunit increases affinity of remaining subunits (T-state → R-state transition)
- Produces a **sigmoidal O₂-dissociation curve** (vs. hyperbolic for myoglobin)

**Bohr effect — factors that shift curve RIGHT (↓ O₂ affinity → more O₂ released):**

| Factor | Direction | Physiological meaning |
|--------|-----------|----------------------|
| ↑ CO₂ | Right shift | Active tissues release more O₂ |
| ↓ pH | Right shift | Acidic environment (lactic acid, CO₂) |
| ↑ Temperature | Right shift | Hot, metabolically active tissues |
| ↑ 2,3-BPG | Right shift | High altitude adaptation |

**CO₂ transport (three mechanisms):**
1. **Dissolved** in plasma (~7%)
2. **Bicarbonate** (HCO₃⁻, ~70%): CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ (carbonic anhydrase in RBCs)
3. **Carbaminohemoglobin** (~23%): CO₂ binds to Hb amino groups

**Carbon monoxide poisoning:** CO binds Hb with 200× higher affinity than O₂ → left-shifts curve → prevents O₂ unloading.

## Biological Role

Hb increases blood O₂ carrying capacity ~70× compared to dissolved O₂ alone. Cooperative binding and the Bohr effect enable Hb to function as both an efficient O₂ loader (lungs, high pO₂) and unloader (tissues, low pO₂, high CO₂).
