# Light Reactions and Z-Scheme

**Course:** [[Course Dashboard]] | **Yield:** HIGH ⭐⭐⭐

---

## Definition

The light reactions are the first stage of photosynthesis occurring in the **thylakoid membranes**. They use light energy to split water, generate ATP (photophosphorylation), and produce NADPH. The Z-scheme describes the pathway of electrons from water through Photosystem II and Photosystem I to NADP⁺.

## Mechanism

### Z-Scheme Electron Flow

```
H₂O → PSII (P680) → Pheophytin → PQ → Cyt b6f → PC → PSI (P700) → Fd → FNR → NADP⁺+H⁺ → NADPH
         ↑ light                                              ↑ light
```

**Step-by-step:**

1. **PSII (P680)**: Light excites electrons; oxidized P680 extracts electrons from H₂O (water splitting by OEC — oxygen evolving complex)
   - 2 H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻
2. **Plastoquinone (PQ)**: Mobile carrier; passes electrons to Cytochrome b6f complex
3. **Cytochrome b6f**: Pumps 4 H⁺ into thylakoid lumen (per 2 electrons) → contributes to proton gradient
4. **Plastocyanin (PC)**: Water-soluble carrier; transfers electrons to PSI
5. **PSI (P700)**: Second photon absorption; electrons re-energized
6. **Ferredoxin (Fd)** → **FNR** → reduces NADP⁺ → NADPH

**Proton gradient → ATP synthesis:**
- H⁺ accumulate in lumen (from water splitting + PQ pumping)
- H⁺ flow through **ATP synthase (CF₀CF₁)** → ATP produced (photophosphorylation)
- ~3 H⁺ per ATP

**Products:**
- **Per 2 H₂O split:** 1 O₂, 4 H⁺, 4 e⁻
- **Per turn of Z-scheme (2 electrons):** 1 NADPH, ~1.5 ATP

### Cyclic Photophosphorylation
- Electrons from PSI → Fd → Cyt b6f → PC → PSI (no PSII, no NADPH, no O₂)
- Produces **only ATP** (when more ATP than NADPH needed)

### Noncyclic Photophosphorylation
- Full Z-scheme; produces **both ATP and NADPH**
