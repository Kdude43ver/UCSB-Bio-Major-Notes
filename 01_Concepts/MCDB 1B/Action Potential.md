# Action Potential

## Definition
A brief, self-propagating reversal of the electrical potential across a neuron's membrane, used to transmit information along the axon.

## Mechanism
1. **Resting state:** Membrane potential = −70 mV (inside negative). Maintained by Na⁺/K⁺ pump (3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in).
2. **Threshold reached:** Stimulus depolarizes membrane to ~−55 mV → voltage-gated Na⁺ channels open.
3. **Depolarization:** Na⁺ rushes in → membrane potential rises to ~+50 mV.
4. **Repolarization:** Na⁺ channels inactivate; voltage-gated K⁺ channels open → K⁺ rushes out → potential falls.
5. **Hyperpolarization:** K⁺ channels slow to close → brief undershoot below −70 mV.
6. **Return to rest:** Na⁺/K⁺ pump restores gradients.

## Key Components
- Voltage-gated Na⁺ channels
- Voltage-gated K⁺ channels
- Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase pump
- Myelin sheath (saltatory conduction in myelinated neurons)

## Biological Role
- Propagates signals along neurons
- Triggers neurotransmitter release at synaptic terminals
- Initiates muscle contraction (via motor neurons)
- Drives cardiac conduction (SA node)
