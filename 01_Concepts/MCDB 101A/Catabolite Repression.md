# Catabolite Repression (CRP-cAMP)

**Topic Area:** Gene Regulation
**Lecture:** [[Transcriptional Regulation]]
**Exam:** Midterm 2
**Importance:** HIGH YIELD ⭐⭐⭐

---

## One-Line Definition
Catabolite repression is the glucose-mediated suppression of genes for alternative carbon-source utilization, mediated through low cAMP levels that keep the CRP activator inactive — ensuring cells prefer glucose when it is available.

---

## Mechanism

| Condition | [Glucose] | [cAMP] | CRP state | Effect on Lac operon |
|-----------|-----------|--------|-----------|---------------------|
| Glucose present | High | Low | Inactive | Transcription low even if lactose present |
| Glucose absent | Low | **High** | **CRP-cAMP active** | CRP recruits RNAP → high transcription (if lactose also present) |

### Molecular Details
1. **Glucose → PTS system active → adenylyl cyclase inhibited → low cAMP**
2. Low cAMP → CRP (catabolite activator protein) cannot bind DNA
3. **Glucose absent → adenylyl cyclase active → high cAMP**
4. cAMP binds CRP → conformational change → CRP-cAMP binds **CRP site at −61** on Lac promoter
5. CRP contacts the **α-CTD** (C-terminal domain of RNAP α subunit) → recruits RNAP ~50-fold

---

## CRP is a Positive Regulator
CRP-cAMP is a **transcriptional activator** — it increases RNAP recruitment. This is independent of the repressor; both must be favorable for maximum expression:
- Repressor off (lactose present → allolactose present) AND
- CRP-cAMP on (glucose absent)

---

## Glucose Effect (Diauxic Growth)
When cells are given both glucose and lactose:
- They consume glucose first (low cAMP → Lac operon low)
- After glucose exhausted: cAMP rises → CRP-cAMP activates Lac operon → switch to lactose
- Two growth phases on growth curve = **diauxic growth**

---

## Common Exam Traps
- CRP-cAMP does **not** block the repressor — it acts on a separate site (−61) to recruit RNAP
- Adding exogenous cAMP cannot fully rescue the glucose effect because glucose also acts at the post-transcriptional level (inducer exclusion via PTS); however the exam often simplifies this to cAMP levels
- CRP binds as a **dimer**; each subunit contacts one half of the dyad CRP site
