# Transcription

## Definition
Transcription is the first step in gene expression where a specific segment of DNA is copied into a complementary RNA strand by the enzyme **RNA polymerase**. Unlike DNA replication, transcription does not require a primer and selectively copies only certain parts of the genome. RNA is synthesized in the **5'→3' direction**.

- **Template Strand**: The DNA strand RNA polymerase reads (3'→5'); complementary to RNA
- **Coding (Non-template) Strand**: Same sequence as the RNA (with T replaced by U)

---

## RNA Polymerase Structure

### Bacteria — Single RNA Polymerase
- **Core enzyme**: α₂ββ'ω subunits — can synthesize RNA but cannot recognize promoters alone
- **Holoenzyme**: Core + **sigma (σ) factor** — σ directs the enzyme to the correct promoter
- Most common: **σ⁷⁰** (housekeeping genes); σ³² (heat shock); σ⁵⁴ (nitrogen starvation)

### Eukaryotes — Three Specialized Polymerases
| Polymerase | Products |
|------------|----------|
| RNA Pol I | rRNA (large ribosomal subunits) |
| RNA Pol II | mRNA, most protein-coding genes; has unique CTD tail |
| RNA Pol III | tRNA, 5S rRNA, small RNAs |

---

## Promoter Recognition

### Bacterial Promoters (recognized by σ⁷⁰)
- **−10 element (Pribnow Box)**: Consensus **TATAAT** — site where DNA melting begins
- **−35 element**: Consensus **TTGACA** — provides binding specificity
- Stronger promoters = closer to consensus → higher transcription rate
- The **spacer** between −10 and −35 is ~17 bp (important for geometry)

### Eukaryotic Promoters (RNA Pol II)
- **TATA box**: ~25–30 bp upstream of TSS; bound by **TBP** (TATA-binding protein), part of TFIID
- **Initiator element (Inr)**: Spans the TSS
- **Upstream elements**: CAAT box (~−80), GC box (~−90); bound by general transcription factors (GTFs)

---

## Mechanism: Three Stages

### 1. Initiation
- σ factor recognizes and binds promoter → **closed complex** (double-stranded DNA)
- DNA unwinds at −10 element → **open complex** (~14 bp bubble); requires no ATP in bacteria
- RNA Pol synthesizes first ~10 nucleotides; unstable → **abortive initiation** common
- σ released → elongation-competent complex

### 2. Elongation
- Core enzyme moves 5'→3', reading template 3'→5'
- Maintains ~8 bp RNA:DNA hybrid in the active site
- Adds NTPs; pyrophosphate released drives reaction
- Transcription bubble (~12–14 bp) moves with polymerase
- Rate: ~50–100 nt/sec (bacteria); ~20–30 nt/sec (eukaryotes)

### 3. Termination
#### Rho-Independent (Intrinsic) Termination
- mRNA folds into a **G-C rich stem-loop** followed by a **U-rich tract**
- Stem-loop stalls RNA Pol; weak rU:dA hybrid in template causes dissociation
- No protein factors required

#### Rho-Dependent Termination
- **Rho protein**: RNA-DNA helicase; binds **rut (Rho utilization) site** on RNA
- Rho translocates 5'→3' along RNA, chasing RNA Pol
- Catches paused polymerase → unwinds RNA:DNA hybrid → releases transcript
- Requires ATP hydrolysis

## Prokaryote vs. Eukaryote Comparison

| Feature | Prokaryotes | Eukaryotes |
|---------|-------------|------------|
| Location | Cytoplasm | Nucleus |
| RNA Pol types | 1 | 3 (Pol I, II, III) |
| Promoter elements | −10, −35 | TATA box, Inr, upstream elements |
| σ factor needed | Yes | No (GTFs instead) |
| Coupled to translation | Yes (simultaneous) | No (nuclear processing first) |
| mRNA processing | Minimal | 5' cap, 3' poly-A tail, splicing |
| Termination | Rho-dependent or intrinsic | Cleavage/polyadenylation |

---

## Key Exam Points
- σ factor is required for promoter recognition, NOT elongation
- −10 = TATAAT (melting); −35 = TTGACA (binding) — both required for strong promoters
- Rho-independent: stem-loop + U-run is sufficient; Rho-dependent requires ATP + rut site
- Eukaryotic Pol II is responsible for mRNA; has CTD tail phosphorylated during elongation
- Transcription and translation are **coupled** in bacteria (no nuclear membrane)
- Template strand is read 3'→5'; RNA is synthesized 5'→3'
