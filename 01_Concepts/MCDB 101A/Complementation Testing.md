# Complementation Testing

**Topic Area:** Classical Genetics
**Lecture:** [[Classical Genetics]]
**Exam:** Midterm 1
**Importance:** HIGH YIELD ⭐⭐⭐

---

## One-Line Definition
The complementation (trans) test determines whether two mutations affect the **same gene** by placing them in trans (on different chromosomes) and asking whether wild-type function is restored.

---

## Trans Test vs. Cis Test

| Test | Purpose | Setup | Interpretation |
|------|---------|-------|---------------|
| **Trans (complementation)** | Are mutations in the same gene? | Mutations on different chromosomes | Complement → different genes; No complement → same gene |
| **Cis (recombination)** | How far apart are mutations? | Mutations on same chromosome | Recombination frequency ∝ distance |

**Key rule from NLM:** The cis test cannot tell you if mutations are in the same gene — only the trans test can.

---

## Interpreting Complementation

| Result | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Growth in trans | Mutations in **different genes** (different complementation groups) |
| No growth in trans | Mutations in **same gene** (same complementation group) |

---

## Exceptions and Traps (from NLM)
- **Dominant mutations** fail to complement even if in a different gene — they appear allelic
- **Alpha complementation (intragenic complementation):** Mutations in different domains of the same multi-subunit protein can sometimes complement → appear to be in different genes when actually in the same gene
- **Rule of Two:** In cis tests where only wild-type recombinants are visible on restrictive host, multiply observed frequency × 2 to account for the invisible double-mutant recombinant class

---

## The rII System (Benzer)
- rII⁺ makes small plaques on *E. coli* K12; rII⁻ cannot grow on K12
- Trans test: coinfect K12 with two rII mutants → plaques = complementation → different genes
- Cis test: coinfect *E. coli* B (permissive) → count wild-type recombinants on K12 → use RF to map distance

---

## Dominance/Recessiveness
- **cis-dominant** mutations (operator, promoter): only affect genes on the **same DNA molecule** — cannot be complemented in trans
- **trans-dominant** mutations (dominant-negative proteins): affect all copies of the target, even in trans
- **Recessive** mutations: complemented by wild-type allele in trans
