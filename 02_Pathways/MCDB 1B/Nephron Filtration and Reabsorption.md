# Nephron Filtration and Reabsorption Pathway

**Course:** [[Course Dashboard]] | **Yield:** HIGH ⭐⭐⭐

---

## Pathway Overview

The nephron processes ~180 L of filtrate per day and converts it to ~1.5 L of urine through four coordinated steps: filtration, reabsorption, secretion, and excretion. Each segment of the nephron performs specialized transport.

---

## Nephron Anatomy Map

```
Blood → Afferent arteriole → Glomerulus (capillaries)
                                   ↓ (filtration)
                             Bowman's Capsule
                                   ↓
                        Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)
                                   ↓
                          Descending Loop of Henle
                                   ↓
                          Ascending Loop of Henle (thin + thick)
                                   ↓
                         Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)
                                   ↓
                            Collecting Duct (CD)
                                   ↓
                              Renal Pelvis → Ureter → Bladder
```

---

## Step-by-Step: What Happens Where

### 1. Glomerular Filtration (Bowman's Capsule)
- **Driving force:** Blood pressure pushes small molecules out of glomerular capillaries
- **Filtered:** Water, ions, glucose, amino acids, urea, small peptides
- **Retained:** Blood cells, large proteins (albumin), lipids
- **GFR:** ~125 mL/min (180 L/day)

### 2. Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT)
- **Reabsorbs ~65%** of filtered Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, water
- **Obligatory reabsorption:** All glucose and amino acids (secondary active transport via Na⁺ cotransporters)
- **Secretes:** H⁺, drugs, toxins, creatinine
- Osmolarity constant (isosmotic reabsorption)

### 3. Loop of Henle (countercurrent multiplier)

| Segment | Permeable to | Transport |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| Descending limb | Water (AQP1) | Water leaves → filtrate concentrated |
| Thin ascending limb | NaCl | NaCl diffuses out passively |
| Thick ascending limb | NaCl (not water) | NKCC2 actively pumps Na⁺, K⁺, 2Cl⁻ out → filtrate diluted |

- Creates **medullary osmotic gradient**: ~300 mOsm at cortex → ~1200 mOsm at medulla tip

### 4. Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT)
- **Fine-tuning** of Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ reabsorption
- **Aldosterone target**: ↑ ENaC and Na⁺/K⁺ ATPase → more Na⁺ in, K⁺ secreted
- **PTH**: stimulates Ca²⁺ reabsorption

### 5. Collecting Duct (CD)
- **ADH** (antidiuretic hormone):
  - Present: aquaporin-2 inserted → water reabsorbed → concentrated urine (up to ~1200 mOsm)
  - Absent: no water reabsorption → dilute urine (~50–100 mOsm)
- **Aldosterone**: ↑ Na⁺ reabsorption in principal cells
- **Acid-base balance**: H⁺ secretion or HCO₃⁻ reabsorption by intercalated cells

---

## Hormonal Summary

| Hormone | Segment | Effect |
|---------|---------|--------|
| ADH | CD | ↑ water reabsorption (AQP-2) |
| Aldosterone | DCT, CD | ↑ Na⁺ reabsorption, K⁺ secretion |
| ANP | DCT, CD | ↓ Na⁺ reabsorption (opposes aldosterone) |
| PTH | DCT | ↑ Ca²⁺ reabsorption |
| Angiotensin II | PCT | ↑ Na⁺ reabsorption; also constricts efferent arteriole |
