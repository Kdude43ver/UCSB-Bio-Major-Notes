# Pathway: Phloem Transport (Pressure-Flow Model)

**Type:** Mass flow | **System:** Plant Transport | **Module:** [[Plant Transport]]

---

## Full Pathway

```
SOURCE (e.g., photosynthesizing leaf)
       ↓
Sucrose actively loaded into sieve tube elements
  (via H⁺-ATPase pump: H⁺ pumped out → sucrose/H⁺ co-transporter brings sucrose in)
       ↓
High sucrose concentration in phloem sieve tubes
       ↓
Water enters phloem by OSMOSIS (from xylem or surrounding tissue)
       ↓
HIGH TURGOR PRESSURE at source end
       ↓
Mass flow of phloem sap (sucrose + water) from source → sink
  (driven by pressure gradient, not pumps)
       ↓
SINK (e.g., root, fruit, growing tip)
       ↓
Sucrose unloaded from sieve tubes (used or stored)
       ↓
Water exits phloem (back to xylem or tissues)
       ↓
LOW PRESSURE at sink end
       ↓
Pressure gradient maintained → continuous flow
```

---

## Xylem vs. Phloem Comparison

| Feature | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Water + minerals | Sucrose + amino acids |
| Direction | Roots → leaves (one-way) | Source → sink (bidirectional) |
| Cells | Dead (vessel elements, tracheids) | Living (sieve tube elements + companion cells) |
| Driving force | Transpiration pull (tension) | Pressure gradient (turgor) |
| Energy requirement | Passive (uses transpiration) | Active at loading step (H⁺-ATPase) |

---

## Vanadate Inhibition (Exam Question Type)

> "Vanadate inhibits H⁺-ATPase. What happens to the plant?"

```
H⁺-ATPase inhibited
       ↓
1. Cannot establish H⁺ gradient for mineral uptake into xylem
   → Reduced mineral absorption → reduced growth
       ↓
2. Cannot load sucrose into phloem
   → Sugars accumulate in leaves; not transported to roots/fruits/growing tips
   → Reduced growth and reproduction
```

---

## Exam Checkpoints ✅

- [ ] Sucrose loaded at SOURCE (not sink)
- [ ] Loading is ACTIVE (requires H⁺-ATPase) — this is where energy is spent
- [ ] Mass flow is PASSIVE (pressure-driven)
- [ ] Phloem cells are ALIVE (unlike xylem)
- [ ] Phloem flows bidirectionally (unlike xylem which is always root → leaf)
