## 🧠 Core Concepts

### Chloroplast Structure

| Compartment | Function |
|---|---|
| **Thylakoid membranes** | Site of **light reactions** — electron transport, ATP synthesis |
| **Stroma** | Site of **dark reactions** (Calvin cycle) — CO₂ fixation |
| **Grana** | Stacks of thylakoid discs |

### Part 1: Light Reactions (Z-Scheme)

**Overall goal:** Use light energy to make **ATP** and **NADPH**.

**Photosystems:**
- **Photosystem II (PS II):** Absorbs light → excited electron donated to ETC → water (H₂O) is **split** → releases O₂ (byproduct!) and H⁺
- **Photosystem I (PS I):** Accepts electron from ETC → re-energized by light → reduces NADP⁺ → **NADPH** produced

**The Z-Scheme (electron flow):**
```
H₂O → PS II → [Electron Transport Chain] → PS I → NADPH
         ↓ (electron lost from Chl)           ↑ (re-energized by light)
         H⁺ pumped into thylakoid lumen → drives ATP synthase
```

**ATP production:**
- As electrons move through the ETC, protons (H⁺) are pumped into the **thylakoid lumen**
- H⁺ flows back out through **ATP synthase** (chemiosmosis) → **ATP** generated (**photophosphorylation**)

**Products of light reactions:**
- **ATP**
- **NADPH**
- **O₂** (released as waste from water splitting)

> ⚠️ **O₂ is produced at PS II**, not PS I. O₂ is a **byproduct** of splitting water to replace the electron lost by chlorophyll.

**Cyclic photophosphorylation:**
- Only PS I is used → electrons cycle back → makes **ATP but NOT NADPH**
- Used to balance the ATP:NADPH ratio (dark reactions use more ATP than NADPH)

### Part 2: Dark Reactions — Calvin Cycle

**Location:** Stroma of chloroplast
**Overall goal:** Use ATP + NADPH from light reactions to fix CO₂ into sugar (G3P)

**Three Stages:**

#### Stage 1: Carbon Fixation
- CO₂ + **RuBP** (5-carbon) → **two 3-phosphoglycerate (3-PGA)** molecules
- Enzyme: **RuBisCo** (ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase/oxygenase)

#### Stage 2: Reduction (and sugar production)
- 3-PGA → **G3P** (glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate)
- Uses **ATP** and **NADPH** from light reactions
- G3P can be used to build glucose and other organic molecules

#### Stage 3: Regeneration of RuBP
- Most G3P used to regenerate **RuBP** (the CO₂ acceptor) using ATP
- Keeps the cycle running

**Net:** For every 3 CO₂ fixed → 1 G3P net gain (rest used to regenerate RuBP)

### The Problem with RuBisCo

RuBisCo can react with either **CO₂** or **O₂** (it's "promiscuous"):
- **CO₂ → carbon fixation (good)**
- **O₂ → photorespiration (wasteful!)** — breaks down organic compounds without making ATP

**This is a problem when:**
- CO₂ is low / O₂ is high (e.g., hot, dry conditions when stomata close)

### C4 and CAM Plants: Solutions to RuBisCo's Problem

| Strategy | Mechanism | Example Plants |
|---|---|---|
| **C3** | No special adaptation; Calvin cycle directly | Most plants (wheat, rice, oaks) |
| **C4** | Spatial separation: CO₂ pre-fixed in **mesophyll** as 4C compound (oxaloacetate) → transported to **bundle sheath cells** where RuBisCo is sheltered from O₂ | Corn (maize), sugarcane, sorghum |
| **CAM** | Temporal separation: stomata open **at night** to fix CO₂ into 4C acids (stored in vacuole) → released to RuBisCo during the **day** when stomata closed | Cacti, succulents, pineapple |

> ⚠️ **C4 separates pathways spatially (two cell layers). CAM separates pathways temporally (night vs. day).** This is a classic exam comparison.

### Absorption and Action Spectra
- **Absorption spectrum:** Which wavelengths are absorbed by a pigment
- **Action spectrum:** Which wavelengths drive photosynthesis
- **Chlorophylls a and b:** Absorb mainly red and blue light; reflect green (why plants look green)
- **Carotenoids:** Accessory pigments; absorb blue-green; broaden the range of absorbed wavelengths
