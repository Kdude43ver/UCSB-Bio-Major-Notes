## 🧠 Core Concepts

### Why Excretion?

Animals must remove:
- **Nitrogenous wastes:** from amino acid/nucleic acid metabolism
  - **Ammonia (NH₃):** toxic; aquatic animals excrete directly
  - **Urea:** less toxic; mammals convert ammonia → urea (liver)
  - **Uric acid:** nearly insoluble; birds, reptiles — conserves water
- **CO₂:** via respiration
- **Excess salts and water**

### Four Steps of Kidney Function

1. **Filtration** — blood filtered at the glomerulus into Bowman's capsule → filtrate
2. **Reabsorption** — useful molecules (glucose, amino acids, salts, water) returned to blood from tubule
3. **Secretion** — additional wastes and H⁺ added FROM blood INTO filtrate
4. **Excretion** — concentrated urine exits as processed filtrate

### Nephron Anatomy

```
Bowman's Capsule → Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT) → Loop of Henle →
Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT) → Collecting Duct → Ureter → Bladder
```

| Region | Key Function |
|---|---|
| **Glomerulus** | Pressure filtration — small molecules pass; large proteins and cells stay |
| **Bowman's Capsule** | Collects filtrate from glomerulus |
| **PCT (Proximal CT)** | Bulk reabsorption: glucose, amino acids, ions, ~65% water (all glucose reabsorbed here!) |
| **Loop of Henle** | Creates osmotic gradient in medulla for concentrating urine |
| **DCT (Distal CT)** | Fine-tuning of ion balance; site of aldosterone action |
| **Collecting Duct** | Water reabsorption regulated by ADH; urea recycling |

### Loop of Henle (Counter-Current Multiplier)

**Descending limb:**
- Highly **permeable to water**, NOT to ions
- Water exits by osmosis → filtrate becomes more concentrated

**Ascending limb:**
- Permeable to **NaCl** (not water at thick segment)
- NaCl actively pumped out → builds up concentration gradient in medulla

**Result:** The medulla becomes increasingly hyperosmotic from cortex to inner medulla → allows urine concentration.

### Hormonal Regulation

| Hormone | Source | Action | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ADH (vasopressin)** | Posterior pituitary | Inserts aquaporins into collecting duct | ↑ water reabsorption → concentrated urine |
| **Aldosterone** | Adrenal cortex | Increases Na⁺ channels/pumps in DCT | ↑ Na⁺ (and water) reabsorption → ↑ blood pressure |
| **ANP (atrial natriuretic peptide)** | Heart atria | Inhibits Na⁺ reabsorption | ↓ blood pressure (opposes aldosterone) |
| **Renin-angiotensin system** | Kidney (JGA) → liver → lung | Ultimately produces angiotensin II → aldosterone release | ↑ blood pressure |

> ⚠️ **Exam Trap:** ADH increases water reabsorption → produces **less, more concentrated** urine. Absence of ADH → dilute urine (diabetes insipidus).
