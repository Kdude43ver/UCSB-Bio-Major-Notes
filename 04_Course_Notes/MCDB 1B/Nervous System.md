## 🧠 Core Concepts

### Two Cell Types

| Cell | Function |
|---|---|
| **Neurons** | Receive, encode, and transmit information via electrical impulses (action potentials) and chemical signals (neurotransmitters) |
| **Glial Cells** | Protect, support, and nourish neurons; oligodendrocytes myelinate axons |

### Neuron Anatomy

- **Cell body (soma):** Contains nucleus and most organelles
- **Dendrites:** Receive incoming signals (neurotransmitters bind here)
- **Axon:** Conducts electrical impulse away from the cell body
- **Axon terminals (synaptic boutons):** Release neurotransmitters

### Resting Membrane Potential (−70 mV)

The inside of the neuron is **negative** relative to outside.

**Why?**
- High [K⁺] inside; high [Na⁺] outside
- Na⁺/K⁺ pump: pumps **3 Na⁺ OUT** and **2 K⁺ IN** (active transport, uses ATP)
- Net negative charge inside at rest

> ⚠️ **Exam Trap:** "In what direction does the sodium-potassium pump push ions?"
> **Answer: Na⁺ out of cell, K⁺ into cell** (not the other way around!)

### Action Potential

A series of rapid changes in membrane potential that propagates along the axon.

**Phases:**

| Phase | What Happens | Voltage |
|---|---|---|
| **Resting** | Na⁺/K⁺ leak channels maintain −70 mV | −70 mV |
| **Depolarization** | Threshold reached → voltage-gated Na⁺ channels OPEN → Na⁺ rushes IN | −70 → +50 mV |
| **Repolarization** | Na⁺ channels inactivate; K⁺ channels OPEN → K⁺ rushes OUT | +50 → −70 mV |
| **Hyperpolarization** | K⁺ channels slow to close; briefly more negative than resting | dips below −70 mV |
| **Return to resting** | Na⁺/K⁺ pump restores ion gradients | −70 mV |

**Refractory period:** After an action potential fires, the axon cannot fire again immediately (absolute refractory) or requires a stronger stimulus (relative refractory).

**All-or-nothing principle:** An action potential is either fully triggered or not triggered at all — the stimulus must reach threshold.

### Synapse and Neurotransmission

1. Action potential reaches **axon terminal**
2. Voltage-gated **Ca²⁺ channels open** → Ca²⁺ flows in
3. Vesicles containing **neurotransmitters** fuse with the presynaptic membrane
4. Neurotransmitters released into the **synaptic cleft**
5. Neurotransmitters bind to **receptors on the postsynaptic membrane**
6. Triggers new electrical signal in postsynaptic cell (or inhibits it)

**Types of signals:**
- **Excitatory (EPSP):** Depolarizes postsynaptic membrane (closer to threshold)
- **Inhibitory (IPSP):** Hyperpolarizes postsynaptic membrane (further from threshold)

### Nervous System Organization

| Division | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **CNS** (Central NS) | Brain + spinal cord | Processing, storage, retrieval |
| **PNS** (Peripheral NS) | Outside CNS | Sensory input + motor output |
