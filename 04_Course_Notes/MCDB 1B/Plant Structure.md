## 🧠 Core Concepts

### Plant Cell vs. Animal Cell

| Feature | Plant Cell | Animal Cell |
|---|---|---|
| **Cell wall** | Yes (cellulose) — provides structural support | No |
| **Chloroplast** | Yes — photosynthesis | No |
| **Central vacuole** | Yes — stores water/nutrients, regulates cell volume | No (or small) |
| **Plasmodesmata** | Yes — channels between cells | No (gap junctions instead) |
| **Centrosome** | No | Yes |
| **Lysosomes** | Usually no (vacuole fills this role) | Yes |

> ⚠️ **Exam Question (W25, Midterm 1):** "The _______ is involved in photosynthesis, ______ provides structural support, and ______ regulates cell volume and stores water."
> **Answer: Chloroplast, cell wall, central vacuole**

### Three Major Plant Tissue Types

| Tissue | Location | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Dermal** | Outer surface | Protection from water loss, pathogens; includes stomata (guard cells) |
| **Ground** | Between dermal and vascular | Photosynthesis, storage (parenchyma), support (collenchyma, sclerenchyma) |
| **Vascular** | Interior, running through plant | Transport (xylem: water/minerals upward; phloem: sugars) |

These three tissue types are **arranged concentrically** and run continuously through the entire plant body.

### Plant Body Plan

- **Shoot system:** Stem + leaves + reproductive structures (above ground)
- **Root system:** Roots (below ground, anchorage, absorption)
- **Node:** Where leaves attach to stem
- **Internode:** Stem segment between nodes
- **Meristems:** Regions of actively dividing, undifferentiated cells (like animal "stem cells")
  - Nearly all living plant cells are **totipotent** — can regenerate a whole plant

### Cell Wall Structure

- Made of **cellulose microfibrils** embedded in a polysaccharide matrix
- **Primary cell wall:** Flexible; growing cells
- **Secondary cell wall:** Rigid; deposited inside primary wall in mature cells
- **Important:** Cell wall constrains cell expansion → governs plant form
- Constrains the **apoplastic pathway** (space between cells and within walls)

### Plasmodesmata

- Channels through the cell wall connecting adjacent plant cells
- Allow direct symplastic communication (cytoplasm to cytoplasm)
- Important for signaling and nutrient movement

### Meristems and Growth

| Meristem | Location | Growth Type |
|---|---|---|
| **Apical meristems** | Root tips + shoot tips | **Primary growth** — elongation (increased height/length) |
| **Lateral meristems** | Vascular cambium + cork cambium | **Secondary growth** — increased width/girth |

- **Vascular cambium** → produces secondary xylem (wood) and secondary phloem
- **Cork cambium** → produces cork (outer bark)

> ⚠️ Trees undergo secondary growth (lateral meristems). Monocots (grasses) typically do NOT have secondary growth.
