## 🧠 Core Concepts

### Plant Hormones Overview

Plant hormones are signaling molecules produced in **low concentrations** by one part of the plant → transported to other parts → bind receptors → trigger responses.

| Hormone | Primary Effects | Key Associations |
|---|---|---|
| **Auxin (IAA)** | Cell elongation; phototropism; gravitropism; apical dominance; fruit development | Produced in shoot tips; moves away from light → bending toward light |
| **Gibberellins (GA)** | Stem elongation; seed germination; fruit growth | "Bolting" — extreme stem elongation |
| **Cytokinins** | Cell division; delay senescence (aging); promotes lateral bud growth (opposes apical dominance) | Produced in roots |
| **Ethylene (C₂H₄)** | Fruit ripening; leaf/petal senescence (drop); response to stress | Gas! — diffuses through air; "one bad apple" ripens others |
| **Abscisic Acid (ABA)** | Stomatal closure (drought response); seed dormancy; inhibits germination | "Stress hormone" |

### Auxin and Phototropism

**Phototropism:** Growth toward light

1. Light hits one side of shoot tip
2. Auxin migrates AWAY from the lit side → accumulates on the **shaded side**
3. Higher auxin on shaded side → cells on that side **elongate more**
4. Shoot bends **toward the light**

**Mechanism of auxin-induced elongation:**
1. Auxin binds receptor → activates H⁺-ATPase pump → acidifies cell wall
2. Low pH activates **expansins** (proteins that loosen cell wall)
3. Cell wall loosens → water enters by osmosis → cell expands (elongation)

**Gravitropism:** Growth response to gravity
- Shoots grow UP (negative gravitropism)
- Roots grow DOWN (positive gravitropism)
- Starch-filled amyloplasts (statoliths) sediment to the bottom of cells → redistribution of auxin

### Phytochrome and Light Detection

**Phytochrome** is a photoreceptor that exists in two interconvertible forms:

| Form | Absorbs | Effect | Stable when? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pr (red-absorbing form)** | Red light (660 nm) → converts to Pfr | Inactive | Stable in dark |
| **Pfr (far-red-absorbing form)** | Far-red light (730 nm) → converts back to Pr | Active (promotes germination, flowering) | Unstable in dark |

**Key principle:**
- **Red light → Pfr** (active) → promotes germination, seed sprouting, de-etiolation
- **Far-red light → Pr** (inactive)
- In daylight: Pfr accumulates (red light dominant)
- At night: Pfr slowly reverts to Pr

> ⚠️ **Exam question format:** "A seed is exposed to red light, then far-red light, then red light. What form of phytochrome is present at the end, and will the seed germinate?"
> Each red light converts to Pfr; each far-red converts to Pr. Last light exposure determines final form.
> **Last red → Pfr → germination occurs**
