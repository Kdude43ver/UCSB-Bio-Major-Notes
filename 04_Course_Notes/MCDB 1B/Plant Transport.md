## 🧠 Core Concepts

### Water Potential (Ψ)

**Ψ = Ψs + Ψp**

- **Ψ (water potential):** Tendency of water to move from one region to another. Water moves from **high Ψ to low Ψ** (always!).
- **Ψs (solute potential):** Always **negative** (solutes lower water potential). Ψs = −RTcS
- **Ψp (pressure potential):** Can be positive (turgor/pressure) or negative (tension)
- **Pure water:** Ψ = 0

**Key rule:** Water always moves from **higher Ψ → lower Ψ** (from less concentrated to more concentrated solution, or toward lower pressure).

### Water Pathway from Soil to Leaves

**Two pathways in root:**
- **Apoplastic pathway:** Water moves through **cell walls and intercellular spaces** (no membranes crossed)
- **Symplastic pathway:** Water moves through **cytoplasm** via plasmodesmata

**The Casparian Strip (endodermis):**
- A waxy (suberin) band around endodermal cells that **blocks** the apoplastic pathway
- Forces ALL water and ions to cross the **plasma membrane** (symplastic)
- Allows selective uptake of ions against their gradients (active transport)

> ⚠️ This is why plants can accumulate specific minerals even when they are in low concentration in the soil.

### Water Ascent in the Xylem (TACT mechanism)

Water moves upward against gravity via:

1. **Transpiration (T):** Water evaporates from leaves → lower Ψ in leaf mesophyll
2. **Adhesion (A):** Water sticks to xylem cell walls
3. **Cohesion (C):** Water molecules stick to each other (hydrogen bonds) → form a continuous column
4. **Tension (T):** Transpiration pulls the water column upward (negative pressure)

**Xylem vessels** are dead at maturity (only cell walls remain) — this allows water to flow freely.

### Stomata and Transpiration Control

- **Stomata:** Pores in leaf epidermis (surrounded by **guard cells**)
- **Open:** Guard cells take up K⁺ → water follows (osmosis) → guard cells become turgid → pore opens → CO₂ enters, water exits
- **Closed:** Guard cells lose K⁺ → water leaves → cells become flaccid → pore closes
- **ABA (abscisic acid):** Drought hormone → causes stomata to CLOSE

### Phloem Transport (Pressure-Flow Model)

Phloem transports **sugars (sucrose)** and other organic molecules **from source to sink**.

- **Source:** Where sugar is produced (photosynthesizing leaves)
- **Sink:** Where sugar is consumed/stored (roots, fruits, growing tips)

**Mechanism:**
1. Sugar actively loaded into sieve tube elements at **source** (via H⁺-ATPase pump creating gradient)
2. ↑ Sugar concentration → water enters by osmosis → **high pressure** at source
3. Pressure drives flow toward **sink**
4. Sugar unloaded at sink → water leaves → low pressure at sink

**Sieve tube elements** are alive (unlike xylem vessels) with **sieve plates** at ends.

### Xylem vs. Phloem

| Feature | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| **Content** | Water + dissolved minerals | Sugars (sucrose), amino acids |
| **Direction** | Unidirectional: roots → leaves | Bidirectional: source → sink |
| **Driving force** | Transpiration pull (tension) | Pressure gradient (pressure flow) |
| **Cell type** | Dead tracheids and vessel elements | Living sieve tube elements + companion cells |

> ⚠️ **Vanadate exam question:** Vanadate inhibits plasma membrane H⁺-ATPase → disrupts ion gradients → impairs active mineral uptake into xylem AND impairs phloem loading → affects both water uptake AND sugar transport throughout the plant.
