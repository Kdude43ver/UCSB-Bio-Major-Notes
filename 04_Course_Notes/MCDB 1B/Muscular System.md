## 🧠 Core Concepts

### Three Types of Vertebrate Muscle

| Type | Location | Control | Striated? | Features |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Skeletal** | Attached to bones | Voluntary | Yes | Multi-nucleate; used for movement, breathing |
| **Smooth** | Organs (digestive, vascular walls) | Involuntary | No | Slow, sustained contractions |
| **Cardiac** | Heart | Involuntary | Yes | Intercalated discs; gap junctions; spontaneous rhythm |

### Skeletal Muscle Structure (hierarchy)

```
Whole muscle → Muscle fascicle → Muscle fiber (cell) → Myofibril → Sarcomere
```

**Sarcomere** = the basic contractile unit
- Bounded by **Z-discs**
- Contains **thick filaments** (myosin) in the center
- Contains **thin filaments** (actin) extending from Z-discs

### Sliding Filament Model

The thin filaments (actin) slide over the thick filaments (myosin) → sarcomere shortens → muscle contracts. **The filaments themselves do NOT shorten.**

### Actin-Myosin Cross-Bridge Cycle

**4 Steps:**

| Step | Event | What Binds/Releases |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Myosin head in LOW-energy state, bound to actin | Myosin bound to actin |
| **2** | **ATP binds** to myosin head → myosin releases actin | ATP binds |
| **3** | **ATP hydrolyzed** to ADP + Pᵢ → myosin head moves to HIGH-energy "cocked" position | ADP + Pᵢ remain |
| **4** | Myosin head attaches to **new actin** site; ADP + Pᵢ **released** → **power stroke** (sarcomere shortens) | ADP + Pᵢ released |

> ⚠️ **Exam Trap:** ATP binds at Step 2 to RELEASE myosin from actin — NOT to power the stroke directly. The power stroke occurs at Step 4 when ADP+Pᵢ are released.
>
> This explains **rigor mortis**: after death, ATP is depleted → myosin cannot release actin → muscles locked in contracted state.

### Ca²⁺ Regulation of Contraction

At rest: **Tropomyosin** covers the myosin-binding sites on actin → cross-bridge cannot form.

When a nerve signal arrives:
1. Motor neuron releases **acetylcholine** at neuromuscular junction
2. Action potential spreads along sarcolemma and into **T-tubules**
3. Triggers **Ca²⁺ release from sarcoplasmic reticulum (SR)**
4. Ca²⁺ binds to **troponin** → tropomyosin shifts → **myosin-binding sites exposed**
5. Cross-bridge cycle proceeds → contraction!

**Relaxation:**
- Ca²⁺ actively pumped BACK into SR (requires ATP)
- Tropomyosin re-covers binding sites → muscle relaxes

> ⚠️ **Exam Trap:** "If troponin can no longer bind Ca²⁺?" → Tropomyosin stays blocking → NO contraction
> "If Ca²⁺ pump stopped working?" → Ca²⁺ remains in cytoplasm → sustained, uncontrolled contraction

### Skeletal Muscle Fiber Types

| Feature | Slow-Twitch (Type I) | Fast-Twitch (Type II) |
|---|---|---|
| **Speed** | Slow | Fast |
| **Fatigue resistance** | High | Low |
| **Energy source** | Aerobic (oxidative) | Anaerobic (glycolytic) |
| **Myoglobin** | High (red) | Low (white) |
| **Use** | Endurance (posture, marathon) | Power/speed (sprinting, jumping) |
