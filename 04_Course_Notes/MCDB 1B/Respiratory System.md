## 🧠 Core Concepts

### Principles of Gas Exchange

Gases move by **diffusion** — from areas of high partial pressure to low partial pressure.

**Partial pressure (P):** The pressure exerted by a single gas in a mixture.
- In air: P(O₂) ≈ 160 mmHg; P(CO₂) ≈ 0.3 mmHg
- In venous blood: P(O₂) ≈ 40 mmHg; P(CO₂) ≈ 46 mmHg
- In alveoli: P(O₂) ≈ 100 mmHg; P(CO₂) ≈ 40 mmHg

At the **alveolar capillaries:**
- O₂ diffuses from alveoli → blood (high to low P)
- CO₂ diffuses from blood → alveoli (high to low P)

At the **tissue capillaries:**
- O₂ diffuses from blood → tissues
- CO₂ diffuses from tissues → blood

### Respiratory Challenges

- **Water loss:** Air breathing exposes surfaces to evaporation
- **Surface area:** Must be large enough for adequate gas exchange
- **Diffusion distance:** Must be thin enough for rapid diffusion
- **Counter-current exchange** (fish gills): Blood flows opposite to water → maximizes O₂ extraction

### Gas Transport in Blood

**Oxygen:**
- ~98% bound to **hemoglobin** (Hb) in red blood cells (4 O₂ per Hb)
- Hb is **cooperative** — loading O₂ at lungs makes subsequent binding easier (sigmoidal O₂-binding curve)
- Released at low P(O₂) in tissues

**Carbon dioxide:**
- ~70% as **bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻)** in plasma
  - CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ (catalyzed by carbonic anhydrase in RBCs)
- ~23% bound to hemoglobin
- ~7% dissolved in plasma

> This buffering system is critical for **blood pH homeostasis.**

### Adaptations

| Animal | Adaptation | Advantage |
|---|---|---|
| Fish | Gills (counter-current) | ~80% O₂ extraction |
| Birds | Air sacs + parabronchi (flow-through) | Efficient at altitude |
| Mammals | Tidal breathing, alveoli | High surface area |
| Insects | Tracheal system | Direct O₂ delivery to tissues |
