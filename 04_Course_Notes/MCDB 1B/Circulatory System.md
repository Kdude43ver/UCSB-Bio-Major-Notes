## 🧠 Core Concepts

### Open vs. Closed Circulatory Systems

| Feature | Open | Closed |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluid** | Hemolymph (same as interstitial fluid) | Blood (separate from interstitial fluid) |
| **Vessels** | Pumps fluid into open body cavity | Blood stays in vessels at all times |
| **Pressure** | Low | High |
| **Examples** | Insects, mollusks | Vertebrates, earthworms |

> ⚠️ Key distinction: In a **closed** system, blood is always enclosed in vessels — it never directly bathes tissues. Exchange happens across capillary walls.

### Blood Flow Through the Human Heart

**4 Chambers:** 2 atria (receiving) + 2 ventricles (pumping)

**Dual Circulation:**
- **Pulmonary circuit:** Right ventricle → lungs → left atrium (gas exchange)
- **Systemic circuit:** Left ventricle → body → right atrium (oxygen delivery)

**Flow sequence:**
```
Body → Vena cava → Right atrium → Right ventricle → Pulmonary artery → Lungs
→ Pulmonary vein → Left atrium → Left ventricle → Aorta → Body
```

**Heart Valves:**
- **AV valves** (atrioventricular): Between atria and ventricles; prevent backflow during contraction
- **Semilunar valves** (aortic, pulmonary): At the exit of ventricles; prevent backflow during relaxation

> Left ventricle wall is **thicker** than right — must generate greater pressure to push blood through the systemic circuit.

### Cardiac Cycle (Electrical Conduction)

The heart's electrical signal initiates each beat:

1. **SA node** (sinoatrial — "pacemaker") fires spontaneously
2. Signal spreads across **both atria** → atria contract
3. Signal reaches **AV node** — brief **delay** (allows ventricles to fill)
4. Signal travels down **Bundle of His** → **Purkinje fibers**
5. Signal spreads through **both ventricles** → ventricles contract

### Electrocardiogram (ECG)

| Wave | Event |
|---|---|
| **P wave** | Atrial depolarization (atria contract) |
| **QRS complex** | Ventricular depolarization (ventricles contract) |
| **T wave** | Ventricular repolarization (ventricles relax) |

### Blood Pressure

- **Systolic pressure:** Pressure when ventricles contract (higher number, ~120 mmHg)
- **Diastolic pressure:** Pressure when ventricles relax (lower number, ~80 mmHg)
- Normal: 120/80 mmHg

**Regulation:** Controlled by heart rate, stroke volume, and vascular resistance. Hormones (e.g., ADH/vasopressin, angiotensin) also regulate through kidney function.

### Capillary Exchange

At capillaries:
- **Filtration:** High hydrostatic pressure pushes fluid OUT of capillary into interstitial space (arterial end)
- **Reabsorption:** Osmotic pressure pulls fluid BACK in (venous end)
- Peptide hormones are selectively endocytosed/exocytosed by endothelial cells

### Components of Blood

- **Red blood cells (erythrocytes):** O₂ transport via hemoglobin; no nucleus at maturity
- **White blood cells (leukocytes):** Immune defense
- **Platelets (thrombocytes):** Clotting
- **Plasma:** Liquid component; proteins, nutrients, ions, hormones
