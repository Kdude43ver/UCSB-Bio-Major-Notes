## 📋 Course Overview

MCDB 101A covers the molecular foundations of genetics: how genetic information is stored in DNA, expressed as RNA and protein, and how these processes are regulated. The course also examines how DNA is damaged, repaired, recombined, and transferred between organisms.

**Core Framework:**
```
DNA → RNA → Protein     (Central Dogma)
DNA Repair ← Mutations
DNA Replication → Inheritance
Gene Regulation → Cellular Responses
```

## ⚠️ Common Exam Traps

- **Complementation ≠ recombination** — complementation is a functional test in *trans*; requires diploids
- **Epistasis = pathway position**, not chromosomal position
- **Pol III vs Pol I**: Pol III is the main replicative enzyme; Pol I fills gaps and removes primers
- **Sigma factor ≠ repressor**: sigma enables RNAP binding; repressors sterically block
- **Lac operon**: glucose present = CAP-cAMP absent = minimal transcription even if lactose present
- **Semi-conservative** does NOT mean semi-discontinuous (these are different concepts)
- **Holliday junction** resolution can yield crossover OR non-crossover product depending on cutting orientation
- **Luria-Delbrück**: high variance = mutations pre-exist; low variance = induced by phage
